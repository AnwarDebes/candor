Interpretability by construction

The explanation is
the forward pass.

Most explanations of a neural network are written after it is trained. CANDOR builds one in: the network reads its input through a layer of named concepts, keeps what they miss as a measured leak, and stamps every answer with a certificate of how much that leak mattered. This one was trained with the CANDOR code and runs in your browser.

How it works

  1. A narrow, named layer. The activation is read through 32 atoms, of which only the top 6 may fire. Training ties atoms to the generating patterns: here 8 atoms match the 8 planted patterns with a mean correlation of .
  2. The leak is kept. Whatever the concepts cannot rebuild is the leak. Here it is about a third of the activation, and switching it off moves the answer by a fraction of a percent: the named concepts carry the decision.
  3. A certificate, not a guess. δ is the total-variation distance between the deployed model and its legible twin, computed with one extra pass. It is a measurement, so it cannot under-report. Mean over held-out inputs: .
  4. Causal by training. The objective swaps leaks between examples and penalises any change in the answer, so the explanation is what the model uses, which is why switching an atom off changes the twin’s count.

Scope, stated plainly

  • These are small networks on synthetic data where the true concepts are known, chosen because faithfulness can be checked there.
  • Retrofitted onto a frozen GPT-2, the method does not beat a reconstruction-only control. Trained from scratch on real text, it certifies tighter than that control (δ 0.47 against 0.65), but the bound is still loose.
  • Completeness is out of reach: some computation always escapes into the leak. The point is that it is measured and bounded, not hidden.
